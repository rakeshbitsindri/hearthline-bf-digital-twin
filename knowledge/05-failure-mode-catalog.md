# Failure modes and early-warning signatures

Use this as a signature library, not as a menu of predetermined causes. A ranked diagnosis has to cite the sensors that match and say which signature was absent. Evidence grades are in [README.md](README.md). Named plant cases with the same mechanisms are in [06-plant-case-studies.md](06-plant-case-studies.md).

| Mode | Mechanism | Early signature | Time scale | What production does | Corrective action used in practice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chilled hearth | Raceway loses thermal connection to the hearth. Iron and slag solidify. Graded from "connection lost, heat remains" to "hearth liquids frozen". About 5 of ~600 furnaces a year, per Danieli Corus. | Falling hot metal temperature and Si. Hearth thermocouples decaying. Tuyere slagging. Cannot raise blast pressure. In the Dunkirk restart, could not reach gas-network pressure. | Hours to days. Recovery with oxyfuel lances is on the order of 200 hours. | Total loss until recovered. | Slow local reheat, staged tuyere opening, high coke rate. Early notice dominates recovery time. |
| Scaffold / scab | Fines plus condensed ZnO and alkali (some silicates melt near 700 °C) cement on a cooling element. The furnace refluxes K (about 10:1 over Na) and Zn. | Stave temperature below expectation. Heat-flux thickness estimate from two-depth thermocouples. One historical case: top temperature to ~800 °F against a 250–400 °F normal, alkali deposits nearly closing the uptakes. | Weeks to months. | Shaft area shrinks, then chilled hearth and tuyere failures. | Cut alkali and zinc input, screen fines, raise wall gas flow, gunning, blasting. |
| Channeling, wall gas flow | Horizontal permeability contrast concentrates gas into a path. | Local stave temperature rise. Shaft-pressure RMS. China Steel No. 2: RMSD of shaft pressure at L7 above 0.15 kg/cm² and annular-gap opening above 60%, giving 10–15 min warning. Rising CO, falling CO₂, higher top temperature, up to about 2 hours before a slip. Rising dust. | Minutes to hours. | Efficiency loss. Left alone, channeling feeds hanging, slipping, and hearth chilling. | Burden distribution, blast reduction. |
| Inactive deadman, lost centre working | Too much ore in the centre. Cohesive zone goes W-shaped. Solution loss makes coke fines. Liquid is forced into an annulus at the wall. | Rising wall heat load. Falling deadman temperature. Weak centre gas. High HM S, low HM C. Hearth-bottom thermocouples on the 1150 °C isotherm. | Hearth actions answer in four weeks or more. | Higher fuel rate, wall wear, shorter campaign. | Centre coke, coarse low-reactivity coke, better tapping. Deadman blowing is used. |
| Hanging, slipping | Overheating lifts the cohesive-zone root, dripping-zone volume grows, gas drag exceeds burden weight. Coke burns out in front of the tuyeres and the void collapses. | Tata BDI: 50 mm radar step is a drop, above 100 mm is a slip, no movement over a window is a hang. BDI of 1 is stable. Weights: drop ×1, slip ×2, hang ×4. Stockrods need ≥2 s sampling. Hot blast pressure rising sharply suggests a hang. Top pressure "bubbling" suggests a pipe. BDI tracks dust load. | Minutes for a slip. Hours for a hang to develop. | Each event costs production. Tata describes industry losses as large. | Blast reduction, distribution, slag viscosity, casting. |
| Low permeability, lost blast acceptance | Resistance rises. High PCI thins the coke layers. Sinter RDI and coke degradation add fines. | Resistance-index distribution shift. ΔP fluctuation. JFE Q-statistic on shaft pressure plus top-gas temperature catches drift that K does not. | Hours to weeks. | thyssenkrupp BF2 was held near 85% of capacity while BF1, on a different practice, held 100%. | Cut PCI and oxygen, retune burden. SIP oxygen pulsing at Schwelgern BF1 moved K down and grew the raceway from 1.10 m to 1.50 m. |
| Flooding and fluidisation | Liquid holdup rises with gas velocity past the loading point. ΔP rises, voidage falls. Classic work: coke fluidises before slag floods, because the liquids are >2.5× coke density. A 2008 study concludes flooding can be what occurs. Report both. | ΔP up at constant blast. Hang with no stockrod movement, then a slip. | Minutes to hours. | Irregular descent. | Less blast, less liquid load, better drainage. |
| Hearth drainage, high liquid level | Deadman plus taphole pressure loss sets the outflow. Slag always remains at tap end. A longer taphole can add ~40 min to a tap and raise level amplitude. A short one can cut ~50 min. | Iron and slag levels from torpedo weight and granulation rate versus computed production. Slag delay. | One tap cycle. | Erratic descent, wall erosion, higher bosh heat loss, higher HM Si. | Online level estimate, taphole length and diameter control. Gerdau: the worst case of bad drainage is a blocked tuyere and a long stop. |
| Hearth breakout, elephant foot | Worst hot-metal wear is beside the taphole and 300–500 mm below it. Elephant foot: deadman core loses permeability or partly floats, and a coke-free gutter forms at the corner. Bowl shape if it floats completely. | Carbon-brick thermocouple step. Masteel A#: 142 °C to 684 °C, 1.5 m below taphole 1. Remnant-thickness models. | Years of wear, then sudden. | Weeks to months down. SSAB Raahe BF2 minor breakout, 2019: ~8 weeks, ~SEK 200 m. | Ti-bearing burden, stop the affected taphole, grout, less driving, more cooling, longer taphole to bend flow inward. |
| Tuyere burnout, water into the furnace | A cracked nose drips water into the raceway. Local temperature falls. Heavy leakage is an explosion hazard. | Top-gas H₂ above a hydrogen balance (Ternium). Per-tuyere inlet/outlet flow difference. Tata leak-detection factor. Normal differential flow can be 0.1–4 L/min, so thresholds are per tuyere. USS BF14: nose-circuit leakage compromised the hearth skull and lifted hearth refractory temperature under all three tapholes. | Minutes to detect. Days if missed. | Forced stop to find the tuyere. Hearth carbon can oxidise. | Replace the tuyere. Hatch soft sensor on 34 nose flow meters at USS BF14, 2018. |
| Stave failure | Cast-iron staves walk. Copper staves deform. Water undermines the hearth skull. | Shell heating. Uneven descent. Stave excursion index. Stops to remove water. | Months to years. | Repeated outages, earlier reline. Ternium Brasil: 6 stave replacements on BF2 against 1 on BF1. | Replacement campaigns. Flow-differential leak detection. |
| High or unstable silicon | SiO forms in the raceway, dissolves into dripping metal, re-oxidises on slag FeO. Dripping iron is hotter in Si near the wall. The tap mixes those streams, and the mix is a large part of the scatter. Higher liquid level means more iron/slag contact and higher Si. | Cast-to-cast scatter. Liquid level. Slag FeO. Tap rate and taphole condition. | Hours. Hearth time constant is 8 hours and up. | BOF cost and variability. | Flux model coupled to hearth flow. Hybrid dynamic models. Short-horizon autoregression. |
| Sulphur and hearth scab | Partition depends on sulphide capacity, basicity, MgO/Al₂O₃, temperature, oxygen potential. An inactive deadman lets metal bypass the coke and miss both desulphurisation and carburisation. CaS precipitation at high S scabs the hearth. Koverhar ran HM S at 0.06–0.10% with high-S oil injection and clogged the skimmer. | HM S is strongly negatively correlated with HM C. Filtered S, shifted 4 weeks, predicted sump-depth change. | Weeks. | Clogging, runner blockage, lining cycling. | Basicity and MgO up, hearth temperature held. Koverhar raised CaO/SiO₂ from 0.9–1.0 to 1.15–1.25 and settled S at or below 0.05%. |
| Alkali and zinc load | K and Zn volatilise above about 1100 °C, form cyanides near 900–1000 °C, condense below about 750–880 °C. Zinc concentrates in the lumpy zone, alkali in the cohesive zone. K in deadman coke ash has reached 30% at LKAB's experimental furnace and starts gasification at 750–850 °C instead of ~950 °C. | Dust alkali and zinc balance. Scaffold thickness. Uptake deposits. | Months. | Scaffolds, weak coke, refractory erosion, hearth cracks (Fangda). | Input limits, slag removal, titanium protection. |
| Cohesive zone after a burden change | Softening and melting temperatures move, so the root height and shape move. | Root position from stave-temperature time-change images lines up with where shaft pressure starts fluctuating. thyssenkrupp BF2: a 2-week ore change plus a sinter-basicity swing (1.9–2.1) produced wall heat load, hanging, and channeling. η_CO fell from above 49% to about 47%. RAR went 510 → 530 kg/tHM. | About two weeks to fully show. | +10 then +20 kg/tHM reductant in that case. BF2 at ~85% capacity. | Pre-qualify softening and melting. Stage the change. Retune distribution. |
| High PCI | Above about 160 kg/tHM: raceway size, local permeability, raceway temperature distribution, coke degradation, and deadman temperature all move. Above about 180 kg/tHM, permeability is the stated concern. Char and coke fines in the bird's nest. Ash raises local slag viscosity. Coke layers get thinner. | Lower-furnace ΔP. Peripheral flow. Wall heat load. Gas-flow fluctuation. | Days to weeks. | Lining life, and a forced PCI cut. thyssenkrupp cut 170 → 140 kg/tHM. | Less PCI, higher CSR, lance and grind. An ACARP review argues unburnt char matters less than coke fines plus slag viscosity. Keep both hypotheses. |
| Coke CSR collapse | Low CSR coke fails under the thermal, chemical, and mechanical load on the way down and the deadman void fraction goes with it. | Weighted CSR and CRI, η_CO, resistance. Prefer CSR above 60 and CRI below 29 for high PCI. thyssenkrupp saw weighted CSR near 63 with large swings on external coke. | Days, the descent time. | US experience of the inverse: higher stability gave lower Si and less Si scatter, fewer burnt tuyeres, longer casts, better bosh permeability, cooler hearth sidewalls. | Blend control, less bought coke, cut PCI and oxygen while quality is poor. |
| Sinter RDI / FeO excursion | Reduction degradation makes fines in the stack. Permeability, ΔP, channeling, driving rate, and CO utilisation all move. Shown for SAIL-Bhilai, Tata, Schwelgern, and Ruhrort No. 6. | Upper resistance rises at very low FeO. Fuel rate and K versus FeO. | Hours to days after charging. | Direct coke-rate and productivity penalty. | Tata Kalinganagar: FeO 10.4–10.8%, RI 73.5–75.5, RDI 28–32. JSW found 8.5–10.0% optimal on its own plant. The optimum does not transfer. |
| Banking, blow-in, restart | Hearth temperature falls 40–50 °C/day on a short outage. Banking of 3–7 days is common. Beyond 7 days is uncommon. Beyond 3 weeks is poorly studied. The usual post-outage problem is a water or air leak that went unseen during the stop. | Hearth temperature. Tuyere slagging. Liquid level. | Hours to weeks. | Ansteel reached design output in 5 days after a 31-day heavy-load stop, using concentrated tuyere opening, 1000 kg/t total coke, slag basicity 1.0, and pre-injected oxygen. | Oxy-gas burner near the taphole after a stop longer than a week. Salamander tap while the liquid is still liquid. |
| Campaign-end lining | Once copper staves solved belly and lower-shaft heat flux, the campaign limit moved to the hearth. Skull grows, then dissolves, and the lining wears in cycles. Tata Steel Europe has said modern campaigns are less predictable and that decades-old hearth design rules look due for revision. | Inverse heat conduction on hearth thermocouples. Two-point remnant thickness. AU-E stress-wave measurement. | Years, with month-scale thermal cycles. | Sets the reline date. | Titanium burden, more cooling, stack gunning, less driving intensity. |
| Hydrogen-rich injection | H₂ burns to steam immediately. Steam drives endothermic coke gasification in the raceway, and the oxygen it consumes is oxygen that would have burned coke exothermically. H₂ specific heat is about 15 kJ/kg/K. Wüstite reduction by H₂ is endothermic; by CO it is exothermic. | Two constraints bind: a minimum RAFT (one study used a 2050 °C floor) and a minimum top gas temperature so water does not condense at the top (one study 118 °C, another 363 K with 20 K margin). | Immediate on heat. Days on the burden. | thyssenkrupp claims up to 20% CO₂ at the blast furnace stage. That is [C]. | Oxygen enrichment restores RAFT and costs top temperature. Heated hydrogen avoids the trade. Modelled coke consumption by solution loss changed only slightly, so a coke-quality upgrade is not automatically required. |

## Cross-signal combinations that separate lookalikes

These are inferences from putting the cases next to each other [I], and they are the combinations the root-cause screen should test before it names a cause.

| If you see | And also | Read it as | Rather than |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top-gas H₂ above the hydrogen balance | Per-tuyere cooling-water differential up | Water leak | A wetter burden or more injectant |
| Top-gas H₂ up | Humidity and burden moisture up, tuyere differentials flat | Weather or stockyard moisture | A leak |
| Hearth refractory temperature step | Nose-circuit flow imbalance | Skull loss from water, then apparent "wear" | Campaign-end wear alone |
| Wall heat load up, cohesive-zone root down, slag FeO up | Wall ore/coke up, pellet moisture up | Distribution shift from wet pellets | A blast-volume problem |
| η_CO down, resistance up, hanging | CSR down or sinter basicity swinging, sister furnace on the same raw materials behaving differently | Raw material, modulated by practice | A generic "process instability" |
| HM S up, HM C down, deadman temperature down | Centre gas weak | Inactive deadman | A sulphur-input problem |
| Averages of blast, top gas, and pressure normal | Circumferential stave variance up, Q-statistic up | Asymmetry or channeling | "Parameters are identical, so the data is fine" |
| Tonnage down | Blast cut was commanded, delay code is steelmaking or ladles | The furnace was not the constraint | A furnace root cause |

## Detection limits at hourly resolution

Channeling RMS alarms (10–15 min), slip classification (2 s radar), and tuyere-leak differentials (minutes) will be invisible in an hourly mean. They reappear, partly, if the export includes within-hour standard deviation, minimum, maximum, and excursion counts, and if stave and tuyere tags are stored individually. Say so on the diagnostic screen when the available sampling cannot support a listed signature.
